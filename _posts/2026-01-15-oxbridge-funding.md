Dreaming of Oxbridge but worried about financing €40,000 or more? No reason to give up.

At the end of this post, I'm linking a huge list of funding opportunities that goes far beyond the usual ones like the DAAD.

## Don't self-select out. Just apply.

Scholarship recipients come and go. Sometimes, no one even applies. The financial situation of these foundations changes every year.

For example, I applied to the Klaus Höchstetter Stiftung hoping for €2–4k. What did I get? €6k, unrestricted, and I'm extremely thankful for this! Of course, nothing is guaranteed. In total, I applied to seven scholarships and ultimately received two. Would I apply again to the five that didn't work out? Absolutely.

## The selection seminars are worth it on their own

The selection seminars alone teach you things you will never learn in any university lecture. You find yourself having breakfast with UN ambassadors, IPO winners, unicorn founders, and many more. You gain insights into topics you didn't even know you desperately wanted to know.

You also learn a lot about yourself. You realise how much you've already accomplished in life and that you are allowed to be proud of that. At the same time, you get humbled. Admittedly, the Haniel selection seminar felt like the Quarter Quell in *The Hunger Games*. Only the best of the best. You see your own limits, but also the darker sides of pushing beyond them. Both experiences are valuable.

## Two more things

Tuition fees are tax-deductible. That means you may get around 10–40% back, depending on your personal situation.

For the MSc in Statistical Science at Oxford, the following scholarships were particularly relevant for me:

- Klaus Höchstetter Foundation (several thousand euros)
- Netzwerk Engagement (€2,500)
- Haniel Scholarship (€12,000/year + €1,500/month)
- Rotary Grants (up to several tens of thousands of euros)
- Hans und Ria Messer Foundation
- Private funding via the Studienstiftung (€5,000)
